# S1: exact int16 accumulator kernels for the owned K4 evaluator

Status 2026-09-27: **ACCEPTED as a pure speed change** (exact search identity,
repeatable 16.2% wall-time reduction). No Elo claim.

## Change

Owned K4 and borrowed Rodent V1.2 share one architecture: 4 mirrored king
buckets, 768 hidden, SCReLU, 8 material heads, QA 255 / QB 64, the same king
bucket table and feature index. K4 stored int32 ("wide") accumulators and
used `VPMULLD` output; V1.2 already had reviewed exact int16 kernels (fused
2/3/4-row updates, variable-row refresh, madd-split SCReLU output). S1 copies
those kernels and their tests into `nnue/ngnk4` (symbols renamed), switches the
accumulator to `[2][768]int16`, and removes the int32 kernels.

Exactness: the loader now proves, for every input bucket and hidden lane, that
|bias| plus the 32 largest row magnitudes fits in int16 (`checkAccumulatorBound`,
`ErrUnsupported` otherwise). Legal boards have at most 32 pieces and one king
bucket per perspective, so every int16 lane equals the exact integer sum;
intermediate wraparound is harmless because add/subtract are exact modulo
2^16. The output fast path keeps the existing int32 `FastOutputSafe` rule; the
int64 fallback is unchanged. Bullet clips weights to about ±1.98, so any net
from this trainer has bound at most 32·505+505 = 16,665. Measured bounds:
archive-A1 e10 15,041; archive-A1 e1 16,172; 20M SF18 K4 5,471.

New regression test: `TestLoadEnforcesInt16AccumulatorBound` (accepts exactly
32,767, rejects 32,768).
